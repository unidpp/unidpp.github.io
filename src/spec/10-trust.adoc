[[cl-trust]]
== Trust and verification

[[cl-trust-markers]]
=== Graded trust markers

Trust shall be graded, not mandated (I9). Every element and event shall
carry one trust marker on the following ladder, in grade order:

.Trust marker ladder
[cols="18,10,52,20",options="header"]
|===
| Marker | Grade | Meaning | Evidence

| unsigned | 0 | no signing evidence | no signatures present
| self-declared | 1 | signed by the claimant | at least one signature, one suite
| attested | 2 | third-party attested | third-party attestation plus at least one signature
| multi-signed | 3 | co-signed | at least two signatures in at least two distinct suites
| log-anchored | 4 | anchored | the artifact is anchored in a transparency log
|===

A marker shall meet a required minimum if and only if its grade is
greater than or equal to the minimum's grade. The marker of an artifact
shall be derivable from signing evidence: log anchoring dominates;
otherwise two or more signatures in two or more distinct suites yield
multi-signed; a third-party attestation with at least one signature
yields attested; a lone signature yields self-declared; absence yields
unsigned. Profiles shall set minimum signing per data class (for
example safety-critical attested; claims may be unsigned); unsigned
self-issued passports shall remain valid for voluntary provenance and
insufficient for regulated claims.

====
Informative implementation reference: `crates/model/src/trust.rs`
(`TrustMarker`, `meets`, `of`).

[[cl-trust-suites]]
=== Cryptographic suites and co-signatures

a) The framework shall support multi-suite co-signatures: the same
 payload may carry multiple signatures side by side (for example an
 ECDSA P-256 signature, an SM2 signature and an ML-DSA signature), so
 that every jurisdiction verifies under its own crypto policy.

b) The core suite registry shall comprise at least: `ecdsa-p256`,
 `sm2`, `ml-dsa-44`, `ml-dsa-65`, `ml-dsa-87`, with canonical
 signature lengths 64, 64, 2 420, 3 309 and 4 627 bytes respectively
 and one-byte wire codes for carrier framing.

c) Jurisdictional cryptographic suites are part of the profile, not of
 the platform: each profile binds the suites acceptable under its
 jurisdiction (including, where applicable, suites standardized in
 ISO/IEC 14888-3, ISO/IEC 10118-3 and ISO/IEC 18033-3, and federal
 suites where so mandated), and migration phases (composite to
 post-quantum) are governed by the agility registry (11.6).

d) Signature framing shall reserve the full canonical length of each
 slot (10.3 c); a slot may be framed only (placeholder) with the
 signature value filled by the signing layer — such framing is
 explicit and degrades the verdict (10.5).

e) Verifiers shall check only what their profile-scoped acceptance
 policy requires; envelope encryption may wrap payload keys per
 recipient under each jurisdiction's permitted key-encapsulation
 mechanism.

f) Attestation representations carried alongside the framework (for
 example the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model) shall be
 admissible as attestation evidence when bound to the trust
 machinery of this clause.

[[cl-trust-stamps]]
=== Stamps

A stamp is a lens-scoped attestation (3, dpp-0128). A stamp shall
comprise at least: the attester; the subject; the subject state
commitment; the lens identity and version; the mode (live or
snapshot); a verdict summary with coverage report; the log-anchored
time; and an optional quantity context inherited by downstream
transformations (9.3 e).

The following rules apply.

a) Stamps shall accrete append-only; a stamp shall be valid under the
 stamper's lens regardless of the issuer's.

b) Jurisdiction stamps on a foreign-issued passport correspond to
 visas; entry and exit stamps correspond to verifier custody events
 (E1 and E14).

c) Condition grading and appraisal verdicts are stamps: value is a
 verdict, not a fact — auction-house lens and insurance lens are
 different lenses over the same scene.

d) Stamp verdicts shall state which verification reading they answer
 (11.4).

Informative implementation reference: `crates/event/src/payload.rs`

[[cl-trust-readings]]
=== Verification readings and verdicts

Verification shall produce graded verdicts, never booleans, and shall
state which of three readings it answers (source invariant 8; the law
needs all three):

a) *evidentiary reading* — what could a diligent verifier know at time
 T, given the then-current trust state: the as-of prefix of the log
 is the evidence, with event totals and counts of corrections,
 recalls, security flags and stamps, and the coverage report. This
 reading protects good-faith actors; their stamps are the proof of
 diligence.

b) *current-state reading* — fraud voids ab initio: the replayed
 status, safety flag and custodian, together with the taint set and
 an explicit voids-ab-initio indicator, prevent laundering fraudulent
 goods.

c) *cryptographic reading* — signature and chain validity alone: chain
 verification, log head, anchor status (absent, matched, mismatched),
 per-slot signature status and the derived trust marker.

A verdict shall carry: the reading answered; the freshness verdict
(10.4); the three readings; the trust marker; and the outcome (pass,
degraded or fail, with causes per 10.5). *Coverage reports* shall be
first-class: the profile's required data points compared with those
present in the render, with the missing list and the coverage ratio;
incomplete coverage shall degrade the verdict explicitly, and consumer
facing grades may be derived from coverage ratios.

[[cl-trust-revocation]]
=== Revocation, retroactivity and distrust

Revocation is not one operation: the reason determines retroactivity
(9.7 b). The following requirements apply.

a) *Prospective reasons* (key compromise after a time, cessation,
 supersession, affiliation change): as-of-T verifications before the
 event remain valid; time-stamping protects them.

b) *Retroactive reasons* (misissuance, fraudulent issuance, authority
 compromised during a window): validity is void ab initio or from
 window start; time-stamping does not protect them. Distrust
 declarations shall carry an explicit window [start, end];
 certificates inside the window are voided, outside it re-validated;
 transparency logs make every anchored artifact's exposure
 discoverable.

c) *Authority over authority.* A superior or quorate body (threshold
 vote under master-list governance, 11.6) may declare an authority
 fraudulent or compromised; cascading distrust follows the window.

d) *Three distrust tracks* shall be distinguished: cryptographic
 revocation (compromise: propagation to transitively bound
 artifacts, emergency revocation), legal withdrawal (accreditation
 removed: trust-list update, profile bindings updated, grace windows
 for in-flight verification) and diplomatic breakdown
 (cross-recognition suspended: verification still succeeds under
 other anchors; affected profiles' acceptance policies quarantine).

e) *Graph taint* shall propagate per 9.7 c; issuer misconduct leads to
 status-invalidation cascades with adjudication, and adjudicated
 outcomes are logged events.

[[cl-trust-signatif]]
=== Trust infrastructure (SIGNATIF)

The trust registry of the framework shall follow the SIGNATIF model — a
federated threshold system rather than a single global PKI — and conforming
implementations of the trust layer shall conform to <<signatif>>. The
normative elements are:

a) jurisdictional trust authorities organized as threshold groups, not
 single keys;

b) jurisdictional trust lists and a globally multi-witnessed master
 list maintained by an M-of-K quorum of independent transparency
 logs;

c) transparency logs anchoring both trust events and passport events
 (seam S6), making every anchored artifact's exposure discoverable;

d) a delegation DAG binding issuer credentials to profile trust lists
 (seam S7), with scoped delegation, emergency revocation and
 revocation propagation to transitively bound artifacts;

e) an algorithm-agility registry governing suite lifecycles and
 migration schedules (11.2 c);

f) dispute resolution and authority-over-authority procedures
 (11.5 c);

g) verification semantics that are coverage-report-based, not boolean
 (11.4).

The semantic registry (6.9) and the trust registry are siblings under
the same ISO 19135 registration discipline.

[[cl-trust-succession]]
=== Succession and continuity

Identities shall outlive their issuers and hosts (I14).

a) *Host succession.* Hosting is replicable by design: resolver
 redirects to fallback hosts or archives; Tier C plus log anchoring
 guarantee recoverable state; the registry marks the host defunct;
 the custodian executes a hosting-handover ceremony; data are
 escrowed per profile requirements. Back-up provider arrangements are
 a special case of this mechanism, not the mechanism itself.

b) *Scheme-operator succession.* Succession obligations (IANA-style)
 shall be recorded at registration; dormant-identifier continuity
 applies.

c) *Algorithm and profile succession.* Post-quantum migration follows
 the agility registry; profile supersessions follow the registry item
 lifecycle; serializations are renders of the core and migrate as
 formats; media migrate through Tier C archival refresh.

d) *Regime succession.* Cross-recognition suspensions and
 reconnections are ceremonies recorded as trust events (11.5 d).

=== Party model and the signing matrix

Trust-graph nodes declare a party class — one of government,
international body, accreditation authority, conformity-assessment
body, manufacturer, verifier, bearer, operator, or update actor —
orthogonal to the node's structural kind. The class states the
node's role; the structural kind states its position.

Which party class may sign which object class is fixed by the
signing matrix, a first-class table consulted at intake: a
segment policy is signed by a government; a profile by an
international body, or by a manufacturer as a self-declaration; an
accreditation by an accreditation authority or a government;
conformity evidence by a conformity-assessment body; a spine root
by the custodian (manufacturer or operator); an interop declaration
by the scheme's operator. An out-of-class signature is refused at
intake with both classes named: a valid signature from the wrong
role is not the object it claims to be. Trust markers remain
graded, never boolean; multi-suite co-signatures verify
independently, and suite-selective verification under a declared
acceptance policy passes.

=== Accreditation liveness

An accreditation is a first-class verifiable object: the
accreditation authority's signed grant to a conformity-assessment
body, carrying the accredited scope, the accepted suites, the
effective window and a version. Its liveness is checked at use
time: a conformity signature whose accreditation has expired
degrades the verdict, naming the authority and the lapsed window; a
missing or unverifiable accreditation is stated, never silent. The
latest admissible version governs; supersession is detectable.

=== Certified hierarchies

Certification hierarchies in the style of CNML — root, issuing
authority, test laboratory, manufacturer model, instance — are
ingested as chains of scoped delegations, each link signed by its
parent and carrying the four-layer scope (authority,
profile-version, product-group, window) plus executable conditions.
Calibration evidence in the style of a digital calibration
certificate — device under test, measurand, result, GUM uncertainty,
unit, interval — is signed by the issuing laboratory. The scope is
checked at every link at use time: an act is admitted only when
every link's scope admits it, and the refusing link is named. An
out-of-scope delegation is thereby rejected when the evidence is
verified, not when the chain is filed.

=== Recognition modes

Recognition of a counterpart's objects is declared per object class
under one of four modes: unilateral pinning under a declared
acceptance policy; bilateral anchor exchange; club lists; and the
master-list mesh. Recognition is class-scoped — a mode recognized
for one object class does not admit objects of another class, and
out-of-class objects are refused with both the counterpart and the
class named. Declared harmonization levels are machine-queryable
per pair and per data class as the minimum of the two sides'
declarations, and drive the per-class behaviour: structural mapping
at L3 and above, document substitution at L1 and L2, and a stated
gap below.
