[[cl-profiles]]
== Profile mechanism

[[cl-profile-concept]]
=== Profile concept and the mount

A profile is a registered, versioned object that binds data points,
transforms, cryptographic suites, trust requirements, access policies,
presentation requirements and a trigger predicate to the neutral core
passport, constraining and transforming — but never redefining — the
single definition of each data point (I2, I10).

Each profile is a projection function of the form
`view(twinState, profile, actor)`, and the projection may transform and
not merely select. The profile interface on the neutral core — the
*mount* (seam S3) — shall be open: any registered lens by any maker
shall attach to it, and no proprietary mount shall be load-bearing
(I11). Profiles, their version supersessions and their trust bindings
(which issuers may attest under them) shall be signed,
registry-deposited, log-anchored objects; verification shall path-find
from any render through the lens and its fact chain to an anchor in the
verifier's own trust bundle, with scope checked at every link, and the
lens registry itself shall be multi-suite co-signed so that every
jurisdiction verifies lens authenticity under its own crypto policy.

A profile shall publish, as mandatory metadata: its version and cited
rules (calibration), its measurement uncertainty (the MTF of the lens)
and its decision rules, and shall be subject to periodic
re-verification through conformance runs (Clause 12).

[[cl-profile-axes]]
=== The three axes

Profile composition shall be three-axial:

a) *jurisdiction* — the regulator whose law applies (for example EU,
 JP, CN, a federal procurement regime, a sub-national state);

b) *sector* — the industry overlay (for example electronics, textiles,
 batteries, construction, tyres, measuring instruments);

c) *characteristic* — predicate-attached (for example conflict
 minerals, heritage status, CITES species content, end-of-waste,
 recycled content, historic-vehicle age).

A profile shall sit on at least one axis; a profile with no axis, no
data points and no suites is not a profile and shall be rejected (6.3).
Jurisdiction profiles bind the cryptographic suites, trust lists and —
where law requires — in-country serving posture of their jurisdiction;
sector and characteristic profiles compose with them by stacking
filters (6.3). Characteristic profiles split into regulatory-triggered
profiles and voluntary value profiles, and shall attach by trigger
predicate on twin facts rather than by law-of-place or industry.

[[cl-profile-manifest]]
=== Profile manifest

The profile manifest shall contain at least the following fields.

.Profile manifest fields
[cols="25,45,30",options="header"]
|===
| Field | Content | Validity rule

| id | profile identifier, a location-free URN | 7.2
| axes | the non-empty axis assignment (jurisdiction, sector, characteristic) | at least one axis
| owner / regulator | the governing body responsible for the profile | registry-governed
| legal basis | citation of the legal instrument or voluntary scheme | dated; used by as-of queries
| trigger | trigger predicate (6.4) | evaluable against twin facts
| min_capability | minimum subject capability class S0 to S3 (6.5) | consistency with freshness (6.5, 6.6)
| freshness | freshness requirement: none, static, or fresh-within window | 6.6
| effective | effective interval, including retroactive applicability flags | well-formed interval
| data_points | list of data-point references (register, item, version) into the semantic registry | non-empty; 5.9
| transforms | registered transforms bound by the profile | 9.6
| validation rules | executable rule packages bound by the profile | 9.6
| crypto_suites | permitted cryptographic suites | non-empty; 11.2
| trust requirements | trust-list bindings and minimum trust markers per data class | Clause 11
| access policies | role-based read and write policies | per-role model
| languages | languages and localization bindings | presentation
| presentation requirements | presentation channel bindings | presentation
| carrier requirements | carrier classes and budgets | Clause 10
| confidential | whether the profile is a non-public register item | 6.8
| resolution | serving resolution scope: none, national, restricted, public | 6.8
| edge_visibility | default edge-visibility class for relationships under this profile | 7.7
| traversal | traversal rights: none, authority-only, role-scoped, public | 7.7
|===

A profile manifest that violates any of the following structural rules
shall be rejected at registration and shall fail validation at
verification time:

* the profile sits on no axis;
* the profile binds no data points;
* the profile binds no cryptographic suites;
* the profile demands bounded freshness (fresh-within) while setting a
 minimum capability of S0 (silent) — an *unsatisfiable profile*
 (6.5);
* the effective interval is malformed.

Applicability shall be evaluated as the conjunction of the effective
interval and the trigger predicate at the evaluation time: a profile
applies to a subject if and only if the subject's twin state satisfies
the trigger predicate and the evaluation instant falls within the
effective window.

[[cl-profile-triggers]]
=== Trigger predicates

Trigger predicates shall be evaluated against the facts of the digital
twin, not against who declares them. The predicate language shall
comprise at least the following constructor set, closed under
composition:

a) the universal predicate `any` (always true);

b) conjunction `all(p1, …, pn)` and disjunction `any-of(p1, …, pn)`
 and negation `not(p)`;

c) fact comparisons `fact-eq`, `fact-ne`, `fact-gt`, `fact-ge`,
 `fact-lt`, `fact-le` over typed twin fact values (string, number,
 boolean, list), where a comparison against an absent fact is false;

d) `fact-contains(path, needle)` — substring test for string facts,
 membership test for list facts;

e) `age-at-least(years)` — a *time predicate*: the subject's age at
 evaluation time, computed from its recorded date of birth using the
 Julian year of 31 557 600 s;

f) `market-status-is(status)` — market-status predicate for
 regulatory-triggered characteristic profiles (for example CITES,
 cultural-goods, anti-money-laundering triggers).

Twin facts used by predicates shall be the testimonial facts of the
twin state (facts, and the date of birth of the subject); they shall
not include actor identity. Time predicates are clock-fired
applicability events: an object becoming older than a given age shall
attach the profile through the dated-binding machinery (6.7) without
human declaration.

====
Informative implementation reference: `crates/model/src/facts.rs`

[[cl-profile-capability]]
=== Capability classes, truth modes and the twin axis

Subjects shall be classified into four capability classes, and profiles
shall declare the minimum class required to satisfy them.

.Subject capability classes
[cols="10,30,60",options="header"]
|===
| Class | Name | Capability

| S0 | silent | testimony-only: no connectivity, no keys, no self-reporting; the twin is reconstructed from testimonies about the thing
| S1 | passive-auth | passive authentication: identity chip, physical unclonable function, or an IEC 61406 identity link; identity attestation without logs
| S2 | logged-contact | dumps on physical read, no communications (for example a battery management system read at a service point)
| S3 | connected | full edge segments: the device commits and serves its own event segment
|===

The following requirements apply.

a) Profiles' freshness and verification requirements shall be
 satisfiable by the declared minimum capability class; a profile
 demanding bounded freshness from an S0 (silent) subject is an
 unsatisfiable profile and shall be rejected (6.3).

b) The twin shall be testimonial by default and sensorial by exception
 (I8): device segments are one testimony class (self-testimony), not
 the foundation of the model. A silent S0 product shall be fully
 servable — type certification, economic-operator passport, custody
 testimonies, recycling events, Tier-A minimum — with zero device
 participation ever; any core assumption of device state is a design
 error.

c) For whole categories the most authoritative facts may live at a
 certification body; custodianship is profile-defined, and
 economic-operator-held and certification-body-held state are both
 legal configurations.

d) Profiles shall declare, per data element, the required *truth mode*:
 attest-sampled, self-committed, or both with precedence. The passive
 twin supplies attest-sampled truth (truth episodic, staleness
 unbounded and undetectable); the active twin supplies self-committed
 truth (truth near-continuous, freshness bounded and detectable, the
 binding question shifted from who saw the object to who attests the
 sensor). Both paradigms shall coexist permanently in the framework.

e) For active-twin elements, measurement validity shall be
 load-bearing: sensor calibration shall be recorded in the device
 registration, uncertainty shall be carried on self-reported values
 per the GUM, units shall be registered (9.5), and health statuses
 that are computed by a model shall be treated as derived verdicts
 whose model is a transform governed under 9.6.

f) The threat model shall be declared per class: silent products face
 carrier-to-object binding attacks (code swap or clone), identifier
 squatting and testimony fraud, with no device attack surface;
 instrumented products additionally face firmware, key-custody and
 channel-compromise threats.

[[cl-profile-freshness]]
=== Freshness requirements

A profile shall declare one freshness requirement:

* `none` — no freshness semantics (archival facts);
* `static` — type-declared static data with document-lookup semantics,
 which never goes stale;
* `fresh-within:{seconds}` — bounded staleness for live views, where
 the window shall be a positive number of seconds.

Freshness shall be evaluated against the projection's as-of stamp
(10.4). Staleness shall be reported explicitly as a freshness verdict
and shall never silently pass (I13).

[[cl-profile-lifecycle]]
=== Profile lifecycle and dated bindings

a) Adding a profile to an existing model shall be a registry
 applicability event with an effective window (including retroactive
 flags), and shall never create a new model identity; a new identity
 arises only on technical type change (7.4).

b) Manifest history shall be as-of-reconstructable: profile-version
 bindings recorded as lifecycle events shall answer the question
 "was this compliant when sold in a given year?" (8.6).

c) Profile versions shall supersede through the registry's item
 lifecycle (proposal → public review → valid, superseded, retired)
 per ISO 19135; profile growth shall be dated binding, not new
 identity.

d) Disagreement between profiles shall be resolved to canonical facts
 plus rules, never to one "true" classification: the canonical layer
 separates facts by provenance class (declared at type; measured at
 instance per method with uncertainty; computed by transform), and
 profiles declare which classes they accept and their precedence
 rules (instance-measured overriding type-declared, else
 type-declared).

[[cl-profile-confidential]]
=== Confidential profiles, dark identities and traversal controls

Profiles may be `confidential: true` (non-public register items) and
shall carry the following serving and traversal posture:

* `resolution`: `none | national | restricted | public` — the serving
 scope; `none` denotes a dark identity that never enters the global
 resolver and is served nationally only;

* `edgeVisibility`: the default visibility class (7.7) for
 relationships formed under the profile;

* `traversal`: `none | authority-only | role-scoped | public` — the
 traversal rights over edges under the profile.

These fields shall be export-control-aware: defence and sovereignty
tiers (national security carve-outs, dual-use controls) shall be
expressible without degrading the framework's verification guarantees —
dark holdings shall evaluate recall predicates locally under national
procedure (7.7).

[[cl-profile-registration]]
=== Profile registration

Profiles shall be registered in federated registers governed per
ISO 19135, with the register roles (register owner, register manager,
submitting organization, control body) and item lifecycle of that
standard. Profiles shall constrain and add, never redefine (I2); where
regions regulate differently (for example differing restricted-substance
lists), they shall register distinct items harmonized to a common
concept. Each jurisdiction's regulator defines its profiles through the
registry, the profile manifest template and a trust onboarding
ceremony (11.6); the framework standardizes the mechanism, not the
regulatory content.

Data-point references shall be triples (register, item, version),
resolvable per the registry's resolution protocol (seam S4), version
pinned at verification time. Whole semantic models shall be exchanged
as registered, checksummed artifacts; cross-register mappings shall
themselves be registered items; dictionary entries shall be immutable
per version and mirrorable with log-anchored inclusion proofs.
