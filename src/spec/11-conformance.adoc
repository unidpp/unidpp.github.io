[[cl-conformance]]
== Conformance

[[cl-conf-general]]
=== Conformance approach

A claim of conformance to this document shall identify exactly one
conformance class from 12.2 to 12.6 and shall state the seams covered
(5.10). Requirements are identified by clause; a claim of conformance
shall satisfy all requirements of the claimed class.

Conformance shall be demonstrated by testing, comprising:

a) *positive suites* — the property-test register of 12.7 and the
 worked scenarios (composite product with mixed-profile children;
 batch split with remainder; combine with mass balance and taint;
 blind installation; dormant-to-live adoption; offline Tier-A
 verification with stale and fresh windows);

b) *negative fixtures* — the negative test cases of Annex A, which a
 conforming implementation shall reject or flag as specified;

c) *casing and normalization tests* — round-trip of every table-driven
 token through arbitrary casing and separator spellings (7.1 e).

[[cl-conf-core]]
=== Core conformance class

An implementation claiming *Core* conformance shall:

a) satisfy design invariants I1 to I14 (5.3 to 5.7);

b) implement the identifier model, canonicalization and normalization
 rules (7.1), and the passport and profile identifier rules (7.2);

c) implement the identifier lifecycle including dormancy and adoption
 (7.3) and the identity lattice (7.4);

d) implement the relationship algebra R1 to R7 with interval semantics
 and the installation schema including recoverability and the
 identity-continuity rule (7.5, 7.6);

e) implement the event model (8.1), the event class taxonomy of
 Table 1 plus the auxiliary classes (8.2), the append-only log with
 chaining, salt discipline and anchoring (8.3), the status state
 machine of Table 2 (8.4), and as-of queries (8.6);

f) implement the transformation algebra with remainder semantics,
 input references, mass balance and taint propagation (9.1 to 9.4,
 9.7), and the unit registry rules (9.5);

g) implement the profile manifest with its structural validation
 rules, the trigger-predicate language, capability gating and
 freshness requirements (6.3 to 6.6);

h) implement the trust marker ladder (11.1) and multi-suite signature
 framing with conservative carrier budgeting (11.2, 10.3), the
 three verification readings with coverage reports and verdict
 outcomes (11.4), and the retroactivity rules (11.5);

i) reject every negative fixture of Annex A as specified.

[[cl-conf-profile-author]]
=== Profile author conformance class

An implementation or party claiming *Profile author* conformance
shall:

a) author profiles as registered, versioned objects carrying the
 manifest fields of 6.3, registered per ISO 19135 (6.9);

b) sit every profile on at least one axis, bind at least one data
 point and at least one cryptographic suite (6.3);

c) declare trigger predicates drawn from the constructor set of 6.4,
 evaluated against twin facts, including time predicates;

d) declare a minimum capability class consistent with the freshness
 requirement, and no profile demanding bounded freshness of an S0
 subject shall be registered (6.5);

e) declare freshness requirements per 6.6, dated applicability windows
 including retroactive flags (6.7), and — where applicable — the
 confidential profile posture of 6.8;

f) bind only registered transforms (9.6) that publish rounding,
 uncertainty and decision rules, and cite their legal basis;

g) never redefine a registered data point (I2; 6.9).

[[cl-conf-host]]
=== Host conformance class

An implementation claiming *Host* conformance shall:

a) serve renders without acquiring authority: republication shall
 never transfer authority (5.9, I7);

b) serve the three payload tiers (10.1) with the exact Tier-A subset
 of 10.2, mandatory in full, and conservative carrier budgeting per
 10.3 (no silent truncation; over-budget rejection);

c) stamp mirrors and caches with as-of times (8.6 f, 10.4) and support
 resolution via linksets keyed by profile context, role and language
 (5.8, L5; <<gs1dl>>, <<rfc9264>>);

d) accept append-only extension of the event record and never rewrite
 history (8.3, 8.5);

e) honour edge-visibility classes, escrow arrangements and traversal
 rights on every served edge (7.7);

f) support hosting succession: registry marking, redirection,
 hosting-handover ceremony and escrow (11.7 a);

g) where operating a resolver, keep resolution by identity only (no
 enumeration surface) and support federated alternates (10.7).

[[cl-conf-verifier]]
=== Verifier conformance class

An implementation claiming *Verifier* conformance shall:

a) verify chain integrity and anchoring, and detect truncation and
 forks (8.3 d);

b) produce graded verdicts — never booleans — carrying the reading
 answered, freshness verdict, the three readings, trust marker, and
 outcome with explicit degradation and failure causes (10.5, 11.4);

c) evaluate coverage against the profile's bound data points and
 report missing items (11.4);

d) state which verification reading each verdict answers, and apply
 retroactivity per the revocation-reason taxonomy and windows
 (11.5);

e) apply profile-scoped acceptance policies to multi-suite
 co-signatures (11.2 e);

f) honour enumeration resistance: verify by identity, require per-edge
 traversal rights, evaluate recall predicates locally, and accept
 proof-of-binding in place of knowledge-of-parent (7.7);

g) reject the negative fixtures of Annex A as specified (12.1 b).

[[cl-conf-device]]
=== Device conformance class

An implementation claiming *Device* conformance (capability classes S2
and S3) shall:

a) maintain a local append-only event segment and publish commitments
 to its log prefix signed by a device-bound key (10.6 a);

b) never contradict past commitments: reveals shall be
 inclusion-consistent with prior commitments (10.6 b);

c) support contact dumps for S2 operation (10.6 f) and device-served
 Tier-B endpoints where claimed (10.6 e);

d) carry sensor calibration in the device registration, uncertainty on
 self-reported values per the GUM, and registered units (6.5 e,
 9.5);

e) treat usage telemetry as personal data, applying the applicable
 data-protection legal basis (6.5, 7.7 b).

[[cl-conf-testing]]
=== Conformance test material

The following property-test register is the minimum positive test set
for Core conformance:

a) identifier casing and separator round-trip (7.1 e);

b) chain tamper detection and truncation-versus-anchor detection
 (8.3 a, 8.3 d);

c) salted chain verification with owner-side salt store, and
 corruption detection (8.3 c);

d) blind-install non-leakage: serialized blind installations contain
 neither the parent identity nor the salt, and verify under
 disclosure ceremony (7.7 c, 7.7 d);

e) quantity conservation: over-carve, over-consumption and negative
 balance rejection; exact remainders (9.2, 9.3);

f) profile applicability by interval and predicate, and
 unsatisfiable-profile rejection (6.3, 6.4);

g) status transition legality at event construction (8.1, 8.4);

h) Tier-A budgeting against the ISO/IEC 18004 capacity tables with
 conservative projection (10.3);

i) freshness triad (fresh, stale, indeterminate) and static-never-
 stale (10.4);

j) as-of prefix and state-hash queries (8.6 c).

Negative test material is specified in Annex A.

For the *operator deployment* class, the test material is the
operational record itself: the backup sidecar with its per-member
integrity values and consistency point, the drill report (restored
members byte-identical, restored configuration validated), and the
upgrade-rehearsal report (domain readings equal, journals
byte-prefix compatible) — the artifacts <<cl-operations>> requires
operators to produce and keep.

=== Jurisdictional registry operator conformance class

An implementation claiming conformance to the jurisdictional registry
operator class shall satisfy all requirements of the core class and in
addition:

* **Governance.** The registry shall operate under ISO 19135 register
 governance (register owner, manager, submitting organization, control
 body roles) and shall record the governance metadata in its discovery
 descriptor.

* **Signed descriptors.** All service descriptors, item registrations,
 and applicability bindings shall be signed by the registry's operator
 key, verifiable via the jurisdiction's trust list.

* **Enumeration resistance.** The registry shall not provide any
 endpoint that lists, enumerates, or permits traversal of
 identifiers-in-use without a predicate-scoped, role-qualified query.
 Discovery shall be by-identity (pull).

* **Mirroring and availability.** The registry's immutable-per-version
 items shall be mirrorable by any conforming peer, with log-anchored
 inclusion proofs substituting for direct reachability.

* **As-of semantics.** All read endpoints shall support point-in-time
 queries with explicit as-of stamps; responses shall be immutable for
 a given (identifier, as-of) pair.

* **Continuity filing.** The registry shall maintain a current
 succession plan, escrow arrangement, and continuity marking, up to
 date at all times.

* **Federation protocol.** The registry shall implement cross-registry
 resolution (delegation records, cross-registry as-of, identity-conflict
 detection) per the framework's registry-federation clause.

=== Operator deployment conformance class

An implementation claiming conformance to the *operator deployment*
class shall satisfy all requirements of the core class and, in
addition, the operations and durability requirements of
<<cl-operations>>:

* **Deployment as data** — state by journal replay, configuration
  externalized (<<cl-ops-data>>);
* **Backup integrity** — per-member integrity values and a
  consistency point (<<cl-ops-backup>>);
* **Restore safety** — verify-before-unpack, explicit-overwrite-only
  with preservation, no tenant collision (<<cl-ops-restore>>);
* **Append-only continuity** — the upgrade prefix property,
  demonstrable by rehearsal (<<cl-ops-append-only>>);
* **Proven restore** — recorded restore rehearsals (<<cl-ops-drill>>);
* **Transfer artifacts** — integrity-verified bundles without
  embedded secrets (<<cl-ops-bundle>>).

=== Federation classes (F1–F5)

Federation conformance is claimed at one of five classes, aligned
with the harmonization ladder. The classes measure federation
level — what a system interoperates AS — not how much of any
software suite it runs; a system running no UniDPP component may
claim F1 with its own exporter, and a system running several
components may still interoperate only at F1.

[[tbl-federation-classes]]
.Federation classes
|===
| Class | Claim | Machine-checkable test

| F1 | publishes verifiable frozen views | a frozen view published by the claimant verifies under a third party's own anchors, air-gapped: the bundle verifies, every input matches its spine commitment, and the lens re-execution reproduces the issuer-side payload byte-identically (<<cl-frozen-view>>)
| F2 | S13 protocol participant | a request/response exchange with a peer completes end to end: the verifier's signed request and the custodian's signed, journal-replayable response verify, the outcome derives from the governing policy, and the response cites the governing policy identifier and version (<<cl-s13>>)
| F3 | mapping-capable | registered mapping items flow in and out: a tier-1 mapping transforms deterministically between the parties' structures, with declared scope and residual for tier 2, and tier-3 pairs render as distinct items with no correspondence asserted
| F4 | shared-profile adopter | a shared profile is served by one party and consumed by another: the profile's manifest is honored by both renders, and disagreement is stated, never merged
| F5 | full core implementer | the whole of this specification's core requirements, verified by the core conformance test material of this clause
|===

The federation level between two systems is the minimum of their
classes and of their policies — the composition law
(<<cl-ops-grid>>) as a conformance statement: a policy cap cannot
be lifted by any bridge, and no class above the minimum is
effective for the pair.

Class tests are runnable by any third party against any deployment,
including the reference implementation, which self-certifies at F5
in its continuous integration. The test material for each class is
public: F1 is the frozen-view verification command of the reference
CLI, reproduced by the foreign harness air-gapped; F2 is the signed
exchange vector (a verifier-signed request and a custodian-signed
response in the S13-MESSAGE domain), which the foreign harness
verifies and re-derives — the outcome from the governing policy —
while issuing signed requests of its own; F3 is the mapping-chain
vector (tier-1 hops, a tier-2 correspondence, a tier-3 divergence),
which the foreign harness reproduces and applies under the tier
discipline. All three classes are proven by an implementation the
framework authors did not write. The reference CLI runs every
class's claim test through one command — `unidpp conform <class>
<material>` (f1 a frozen view under the runner's own anchors; f2
the signed exchange; f3 the mapping chain; f4 two views under one
shared profile; f5 the family's golden-vector sweep) — so any third
party can test any deployment, including its own, with one
binary.
