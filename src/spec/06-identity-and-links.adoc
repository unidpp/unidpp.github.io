[[cl-identity]]
== Identity, granularity and relationships

[[cl-id-model]]
=== Identifier model

Each subject shall carry exactly one product identity (I1), comprising
an identifier scheme, a key, and optional lot and serial extensions,
with a derived granularity of model, batch or item.

a) The framework shall accept the following identifier scheme families,
 and shall be extensible to further registered schemes: GTIN, SGTIN,
 GSRN, GLN (GS1 keys), CPID (ISO/IEC 15459), SSN, UPU, VIN, Handle,
 DOI, URI, and issuer-local schemes (`local:<tag>`).

b) Wire forms accepted shall include: scheme-prefixed tokens
 (`gtin:4006381333931`), ISO/IEC 15459 / GS1 element strings
 (`01+4006381333931+10+LOT42+21+SN7`, application identifiers 01 key,
 10 lot/batch, 21 serial; 11, 17 and 240 accepted and ignored for
 identity purposes), bare 4-to-14-digit GTINs, and HTTP(S) URIs.

c) The canonical display form shall be the element string for GS1
 schemes (`01+K[+10+L][+21+S]`) and the lowercase scheme-prefixed
 token otherwise.

d) Identifier granularity shall be derived: model-granularity by
 default for GTIN, GLN, Handle, DOI, URI and local schemes; item
 granularity by default otherwise.

e) The framework shall not legislate casing. Identifier tokens, scheme
 names and every table-driven value of the core shall be accepted in
 any casing and separator spelling and shall canonicalize to the
 normalization table's lowercase form; two spellings that normalize
 equal shall compare equal. Casing drift between systems is thereby a
 non-defect.

Informative implementation reference: `crates/model/src/identifier.rs`

[[cl-id-urn]]
=== Passport and profile identifiers

Passport identifiers and profile identifiers shall be location-free
slugs or URN forms, never resolver addresses (I14 — identities outlive
their issuers and hosts):

* passport identifier: `urn:unidpp:passport:<slug>`;
* profile identifier: `urn:unidpp:profile:<slug>`.

Identifier values shall be non-empty, at most 128 characters, free of
whitespace and control characters, and case-normalized to lowercase.

====
Informative implementation reference: `crates/model/src/ids.rs`.

[[cl-id-states]]
=== Identity states and dormancy

Identifier lifecycle shall comprise at least the states *dormant* and
*live*, with the following rules (I1, I3).

a) An identifier may be recorded as dormant for an object class for
 which no passport regime yet exists; dormant identifiers are
 issuance evidence, and shall be recorded at the finest available
 granularity as (scheme, lot or serial, count) even when no passport
 exists behind them yet.

b) When a future regime issues passports for the object class, it shall
 adopt the recorded dormant identifiers rather than mint new ones;
 minting fresh identifiers would orphan the installed base. Adoption
 is the only dormant-to-live transition; identifiers are never
 re-minted.

c) Absorption shall be treated as regime-temporal, not permanent: an
 absorbed component (for example a cell inside a certified pack) is
 modeled as a transformation input (9.3) recorded as a dormant
 identifier, issuable by adoption when the future regime lands.

d) Retrospective issuance shall be provided by one or both of:
 *issue-by-records* (the manufacturer mines build data and issues
 lot passports for historical production) and *issue-on-first-touch*
 (the dormant identity activates at the next service, repair,
 replacement or resale event, at which a carrier may be
 retro-marked). Retroactive legal timing shall be carried by the
 dated-binding machinery (6.7).

e) Unmarked components (for example cells in a pack) shall mostly be
 issued at lot level (pack → cell-lot passport plus count) unless a
 marking rule mandates instance level.

f) Replacement of a physical carrier (re-issuing a QR code on an
 unchanged identity) shall be a registry event on the unchanged
 identity and shall not alter the identifier.

[[cl-id-lattice]]
=== The identity lattice and issuance granularity

The framework shall distinguish manufacturer (an actor, not a level),
model (a registered type definition), type version, configuration and
instance, according to the following rules (source invariants 9 and 10
of 5.11).

a) A *type passport* (model level) holds type-approved facts:
 certifications, type tests, declared values — the type twin. The
 type definition defines semantics for instance elements, fixing
 single-definition reuse.

b) An *instance passport* shall reference its type *and the exact type
 version* (hardware revisions visible, eliminating the silent
 revision problem), and shall override with instance-measured facts
 per precedence rules (6.7 d).

c) A configure-to-order product is model plus configuration vector:
 each option is itself a registered component model with its own
 passport, composing through the same child-reference mechanism as
 any composite (7.5, 7.6).

d) Product line and family are registry navigation metadata, not
 passport levels.

e) One-offs and prototypes shall be instance passports with nullable
 type reference.

f) Regulated modifications shall spawn derived types via events, with
 mandatory profile re-evaluation (8.5).

g) Passports shall be issued wherever a legal duty attaches: type
 approval regimes issue type passports; most passport law issues
 batch or item passports. Model-contains-model defines potential
 composition; instance references actual child instances.

h) A composite product is a federation of passports, not one
 mega-passport: one passport per placed-on-market product identity,
 linked by typed child references in the parent's manifest, by
 containment and transformation events, and by a neutral bill-of-
 materials reference structure. Children join by identity reference,
 never by data copying; roll-up views are computed from references
 (9.1). A child needs its own carrier if and only if it is
 independently placed on a market or regulated.

[[cl-id-algebra]]
=== The relationship algebra (R1 to R7)

Passports are nodes; edges are typed, and the types have different
legal and lifecycle semantics (I5). The framework shall provide the
following relationship classes.

.Relationship classes of the passport algebra
[cols="8,24,40,28",options="header"]
|===
| Class | Name | Semantics | Rules

| R1 | Association | compatibility, supply, same-maker | navigational only; free add and remove; carries no binding
| R2 | Derivation | input → output via transformation events | historical, directional; inputs may be consumed; governed by Clause 9
| R3 | Installation | structural, parent ↔ child, temporal | its own event class, not generic containment; 7.6
| R4 | Type lineage | type versioning, derived types, configuration vectors | the identity lattice (7.4)
| R5 | Profile-of | lens ↔ core | not a passport-to-passport edge; seam S3
| R6 | Custody | social and control edges | orthogonal to structure: ownership changes while composition stays fixed
| R7 | Membership | group nodes: shipments, consignments, kits, fleets, recall sets, batches-as-groups | temporal membership; groups get passports only when themselves placed on market (a kit sold as a product is R3 composition); otherwise logistical and query objects
|===

Every relationship record shall carry: the relationship type, the other
passport on the edge, the direction (outgoing or incoming), the
temporal interval, and — for installation — the binding structure
(7.6). Interval closure shall be explicit (an uninstall or removal
event closes the interval), and activity at a time shall be evaluated
by interval containment, with an open interval remaining active into
the future.

[[cl-id-installation]]
=== Installation (R3)

Installation is its own event class, not generic containment, because
an installed or fastened component is a different legal object from the
standalone one. The installation schema shall be:

`install(child, parent, {method, slotId, pairing, alteration[],
performedBy, at})`

with the components specified below.

a) *method* — the attachment method: fastened, soldered, glued,
 welded, potted, torque-to-yield, coated, keyed, or a registered
 extension (`other:<token>`).

b) *slotId* — the parent-scoped slot identity (for example "battery
 slot 1 of car X"), where applicable.

c) *pairing* — the re-identification mode: `none | firmware | key |
 module` (battery-management-to-VIN pairing, anti-theft module
 pairing, key re-issue).

d) *alteration[]* — the permanent alterations performed on the child:
 soldered, glued, welded, potted, torque-to-yield, conformal coating,
 or a registered extension. Installation may make the prior state
 unrecoverable.

e) *recoverability* — the declared recoverability of the binding:
 `restorable` (the child resumes standalone life), `harvestable`
 (recovered but altered — continues with harvested status and parent
 history), `destructive` (the child ceases; flows to a material
 passport via R2) or `absorbing` (identity dissolves into the
 parent). The *identity-continuity rule* shall hold: disassembly that
 restores a marketable object with identity continuity is R3
 (installation); identity dissolution is R2 (transformation). The
 identity flow consequences shall be: restorable → continues
 standalone; harvestable → continues harvested; destructive → to
 material passport; absorbing → dissolved.

f) *visibility* — the edge-visibility class with escrow arrangement and
 audiences (7.7).

g) *Bidirectional parent knowledge shall be mandatory*: the parent's
 manifest lists children, and the child's event log records its
 installation interval (parent, slot, method) — for recall routing,
 scoped service access (the parent's repairer gets scoped rights over
 child data), ownership transfer (conveying child context) and
 end-of-life routing. Installation history is value-relevant
 provenance: a harvested battery sold standalone carries its service
 interval and removal reason.

h) *Lifecycle coupling* shall be recorded: warranty re-scope and
 service economics ride the parent; the part is no longer sellable as
 new.

i) *Part replacement* shall be an uninstall followed by an install
 (two events, with harvest or destroy outcome on the removed child),
 with the bill-of-materials instance updated; regulated children keep
 their own passports through it, and absorbed ones have none under
 the current regime (issuable later by adoption, 7.3).

j) *Cross-jurisdiction installation* shall be recorded under the
 installer's credentials with both profiles surviving: a composite
 may carry mixed-profile children naturally, by composition by
 reference.

An installation edge without a binding structure shall be rejected; an
association edge carrying a binding shall be rejected.

[[cl-id-visibility]]
=== Edge visibility and enumeration resistance

The bidirectional parent knowledge of installation is a disclosure
hazard: a child-to-parent edge reveals where a thing is — a household
or a site. A traversable graph with entitled upstream observers is
surveillance infrastructure. The following rules shall therefore hold
(I12).

a) *Manufacturer posture.* The manufacturer is a definition-holder, not
 a state-observer. Post-sale visibility into instance state shall be
 owner-push (the owner self-discloses a serial for benefits) or
 authority-based (a regulator compels traversal) — never maker-pull.
 The system's default channels are owner-push; maker-pull shall not
 be a framework feature.

b) *Edge visibility classes.* Every relationship shall declare one of
 `public | restricted (role-qualified) | blind | escrowed`, with an
 optional escrow trustee and declared audiences. An escrowed edge
 without a trustee shall be rejected; a public edge shall not carry
 audiences. Blind shall be the default for consumer installations,
 because the object-to-household linkage is personal data requiring a
 legal basis per profile.

c) *Proof of binding is not knowledge of parent.* The child shall
 satisfy the parent-knowledge requirement cryptographically: a salted
 commitment to the parent, `H(salt ‖ parent)`, recorded in the child's
 log, with an optional escrow envelope under a threshold trustee.
 Verifiable modes shall establish "validly installed in *some* parent
 under profile P" (warranty, compliance) without revealing which;
 full disclosure shall occur only by ceremony: court, regulator or
 consent.

d) *Salt discipline.* Salts shall live only in the owner-side salt
 store or in an escrow envelope — never in the log and never in any
 serialization of it. Salted commitments shall be re-computable only
 under a disclosure ceremony.

e) *Predicate-based recall.* The recall set shall never be enumerated
 centrally: the predicate shall be published and each custodian shall
 evaluate it locally against their own holdings (including dark and
 defence holdings under national procedure) and act; the manufacturer
 receives aggregates or nothing. Recall propagation notifies
 custodians transitively, but through local predicate evaluation
 rather than central construction of the recall set.

f) *Enumeration resistance as a system property.* Resolution shall be
 by identity (pull); traversal shall require per-edge rights; logs
 shall anchor commitments (hashes), never facts, so log operators
 cannot correlate edges; accumulator and blind-membership proofs
 shall give valid-standing checks without revealing which or where.
 No party — including any registry, resolver network or log operator
 — shall be able to enumerate the installed base of anything.

g) *Dark identities.* Identities that never enter the global resolver
 shall be servable nationally only; confidential profiles (6.8) shall
 carry `resolution`, `edgeVisibility` and `traversal` fields.
