[[cl-semantics]]
== Semantic registry and mappings

[[cl-semantics-mappings]]
=== Mapping tiers

Cross-register correspondences are registered under a three-tier
discipline. A tier 1 mapping is deterministic: it names a
registered, versioned transform, and applying that transform to a
source value yields the target value, the same output for the same
input. A tier 2 mapping is a correspondence: it declares its scope
and states its residual — what the correspondence does not cover —
and asserts similarity, never transformability. A tier 3 record
asserts that NO mapping exists between the two items; the pair is
recorded precisely so that no correspondence is ever inferred, and
the two items remain distinct.

Intake and query honour the tiers: a tier 3 record renders as two
distinct items with no correspondence; a tier 2 correspondence
carries its scope and residual verbatim and is refused outside its
scope; a tier 1 mapping applies its registered transform
deterministically.

[[cl-semantics-composition]]
=== Mapping composition

Mapping chains compose per tier: tier 1 chains compose
associatively, their transform references concatenated in order;
tier 2 links propagate residuals, accumulating in order with scopes
intersecting; a tier 3 edge in a path refuses the whole
composition. Chains record every constituent mapping version, and
chain length is capped by policy — an over-cap chain is refused at
composition, stated.

[[cl-semantics-units]]
=== Units, uncertainty and decision rules

Measured values are stored in canonical units only — the unit under
which their quantity kind is registered. A value offered in a
non-canonical unit is refused at intake with the registered
conversion offered; the conversion, when applied, scales the value
and its uncertainty alike. A derived value without GUM uncertainty
is refused. Transforms propagate uncertainty by the linear rule:
scaling multiplies the uncertainty by the factor's absolute value,
and independent terms combine in quadrature.

Classification thresholds declare their conformity decision rule:
shared-risk, in which the measured value alone decides, or
guard-banded per ILAC-G8, in which conformity requires the value
plus the expanded margin inside the limit and nonconformity requires
it outside by the margin, the interval between being inconclusive
and stated. A boundary measurement classifies differently under the
two rules, both correctly.

[[cl-semantics-multilingual]]
=== Multilingual values

Element values are localized per BCP 47 with full tags, script
subtags included. Tags are validated for well-formedness: the
language subtag is two or three letters; the second subtag, when
alphabetic, is a four-letter script or a two-letter region. The
defect of EN 18223 — a language restricted to a two-letter code,
incapable of expressing a script — is refused. Retrieval falls back
by prefix (a request for `zh` resolves a held `zh-Hans`) and states
the fallback taken.

[[cl-semantics-exports]]
=== Parseable exports and stable identifiers

Every registry item and profile exports a parseable,
JSON-LD-shaped document carrying its stable identifier: a registry
URI of the form register/item, optionally pinned to a version. The
identifier resolves back — its shape parses to register, item and
version — so a consumer holding only the export can re-address the
registry. A foreign parser round-trips the export; malformed
identifiers are stated failures.

[[cl-semantics-federation]]
=== Register federation and resolution

Registries federate by item exchange: an item version crosses in
its canonical bytes, and mirrors serve the same immutable bytes —
identity of origin and mirror is proven by digest equality against
the anchored commitment, never assumed. An unlisted version is a
stated absence.

Resolution serves linksets keyed by profile context, role,
language and request region, with explicit per-dimension fallback
(stated, never silent) and as-of stamps for cacheability. Dark
identities have no public entry: their absence is indistinguishable
from an unknown identity, and no error leaks their existence.
