[[cl-discovery]]
== Discovery and onboarding

[[cl-discovery-roles]]
=== The operator role model

Operators act under a role model of thirty roles in four families —
issuance, lifecycle, verification, and infrastructure — each
specified by a fixed rule row: what it may issue, which events it
may append, what it may read, and what it may operate. The catalog
is first-class data; conforming implementations consult it rather
than encode it.

An operator credential binds role tokens and a federation tier.
The tiers are: read federation (any consumer); service federation
(operators register services); trust federation (trust authorities
join the master list by threshold admission); and governance
(registrars and control bodies, with the dispute panel as a
threshold quorum).

Authorization consults the catalog and the tier: an in-scope
action is admitted with its role named; an out-of-scope action is
refused with the action named; an action whose admitting role
requires a higher tier is refused with both tiers named. A
manufacturer issues passports and proposes recalls but does not
enact them; only market-surveillance authorities enact. A consumer
holds no operational action.

[[cl-discovery-onboarding]]
=== Onboarding, seed bundles and listing gates

A new operator bootstraps from a seed bundle: the trust anchors to
pin, the root service descriptors to seed, and the starting role
credential — one verifiable unit. Onboarding is a ceremony: the
credential is checked against the role model and the tier
requirement; the service application passes the listing gates,
being predicates over its descriptor — jurisdiction, residency
class, and a continuity pointer, the succession plan every service
operator registers at admission. A gate refusal states the failed
predicate; the ceremony ends with the service listed and queryable.

[[cl-discovery-peers]]
=== Peer discipline

No registry, resolver or scheme is privileged. Registries federate
as peers: item exchange runs the same operation in both
directions, each deployment serving the other's items, and no
message of the protocol names a master. A registry-operator
conformance contract governs admission: the 19135 item lifecycle,
canonical item exchange, mirror serving of foreign items, and
enumeration resistance on the public surface — each item failing,
when violated, with the item named. A deployment that does not
conform does not federate.
