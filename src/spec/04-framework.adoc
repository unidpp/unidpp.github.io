[[cl-framework]]
== Framework

[[cl-framework-concepts]]
=== Framework concepts

The UniDPP framework models the digital product passport as a
*projection*, not as a static document. The authoritative record of a
subject is its event-sourced change log (Clause 8); the passport is the
legal materialized view of that record under a profile (Clause 6). The
following counter-principles govern every other clause of this
document:

a) specify the optics and derive the image format — the framework
   specifies identity, events, projections and trust, and derives
   representations;

b) legislate invariants, not implementations — casing conventions,
   address shapes and serialization corners are versionable registry
   items, not core requirements;

c) one formalization — the core model is single-sourced; UML, JSON and
   XML renders are derived views and never authoritative over it;

d) versioned lenses, immutable scenes — profiles are versioned and
   registered; the testimonial record is append-only and never altered
   by observation;

e) publish the uncertainty — every lens publishes its measurement
   uncertainty and its decision rules as mandatory metadata;

f) open mount — the profile interface on the neutral core is open, and
   the interop guarantee and the anti-lock-in clause are one and the
   same sentence.

The profile is conceived as a calibrated lens placed on the digital
twin: the scene (the twin, invariant, append-only) is never altered by
observation; the lens (the profile, versioned, registered) selects and
transforms. The lens is not passive glass but a *legal measuring
instrument*: pattern approval at registration, published calibration,
uncertainty and decision rules, and periodic re-verification through
conformance runs.

[[cl-framework-inv]]
=== Normative design invariants

The framework shall satisfy invariants I1 to I14 stated in 5.3 to 5.7.
Each invariant is expressed as one or more testable requirements.
Implementations claiming Core conformance (Clause 12) shall satisfy all
of them. The informative implementation reference for this clause is
the reference core workspace `unidpp-core`, crate `unidpp-model`
(`crates/model/src/lib.rs`) and its modules cited in the sub-clauses
below.

[[inv-identity]]
=== Identity and semantics invariants (I1 to I3)

[[inv-I1]]
*Invariant I1 — one subject, one identity, never re-minted.* The
framework shall provide exactly one product identity per subject. An
identity shall never be re-minted: an identifier once recorded shall be
reused, not replaced, throughout the subject's life, across ownership
changes, host changes and regime changes. Where no passport regime yet
exists for an object class, identifiers shall be recorded as dormant
identifiers (7.3), and a future regime shall adopt the recorded dormant
identifiers (dormant-to-live adoption) rather than mint new ones.

[[inv-I2]]
*Invariant I2 — single definition, many constraints.* Every data point
shall be defined once in the semantic registry (5.9, 6.9). Profiles
shall constrain and transform data points and shall never redefine
them. Inter-profile disagreement shall be expected and auditable: two
profiles deriving different classifications of one canonical fact
constitute two correct projections, not an error. A genuine
same-provenance-class contradiction between values of one data point
shall be treated as an integrity incident and flagged and audited, and
shall never be silently merged.

[[inv-I3]]
*Invariant I3 — finest recorded granularity.* Inputs to records shall
be recorded at the finest identifier granularity available (model,
batch or item). Dormant identifiers shall be allowed at any time, even
when no passport exists behind them yet. Absorbing transformations
shall record their inputs at the finest available granularity as
(scheme, lot or serial, count).

[[inv-structure]]
=== Structure and lifecycle invariants (I4 to I7)

[[inv-I4]]
*Invariant I4 — append-only event sourcing.* The authoritative record
of a subject shall be its distributed, log-anchored event log. Nothing
shall ever be edited in place: every post-first-sale change shall be an
appended, typed event (Clause 8). No single party shall hold the
authoritative record alone; the transparency-log anchoring shall be the
tamper-evident, globally consistent spine. Diverging commitments for
one identity (forks) shall be log-detectable integrity incidents that
are adjudicated and never silently merged.

[[inv-I5]]
*Invariant I5 — typed relationship algebra.* Relationships between
passports shall be typed edges of the relationship algebra R1 to R7
(7.5), each carrying its own legal and lifecycle semantics. Installation
edges shall carry the binding structure method, slot identity, pairing,
alterations, recoverability and visibility (7.6, 7.7).

[[inv-I6]]
*Invariant I6 — state machine legitimacy.* Passport status shall be one
of the normative statuses of 8.4, and status changes shall occur only
through the defined transitions. Applicability of profiles shall be
dated, including retroactive applicability flags. Growth of the profile
set shall never change the subject's identity.

[[inv-I7]]
*Invariant I7 — issuance where legal duty attaches; separable duties.*
Passports shall be issued wherever a legal duty attaches (type-approval
regimes issue type passports; batch- and item-level duties issue batch
and item passports). Issuance, hosting, identity, anchoring, archiving
and trust shall be separable duties: one party may hold several, none
is implied by another, and republication of a render by any party shall
never transfer authority.

[[inv-projection]]
=== Projection and trust invariants (I8 to I9)

[[inv-I8]]
*Invariant I8 — deterministic projection.* A passport shall be a
deterministic, registered and legally cited projection of the
testimonial record. The twin shall be testimonial by default and
sensorial by exception, according to the subject capability classes S0
to S3 (6.5). Transforms shall publish their uncertainty, rounding and
decision rules (9.6). The canonical record shall store measured values
in canonical units only; pre-transformed duplicates shall not be
stored, so that rule changes re-project rather than re-enter data.

[[inv-I9]]
*Invariant I9 — graded trust.* Trust shall be graded, not mandated:
every element and event shall carry a trust marker on the ladder of
11.2, signatures may be multi-suite, verification shall produce
verdicts with coverage reports under one of the three verification
readings (11.4), revocation reason shall determine retroactivity
(11.5), and taint shall propagate through the provenance graph (9.7).

[[inv-sovereignty]]
=== Sovereignty and neutrality invariants (I10 to I12)

[[inv-I10]]
*Invariant I10 — three-axial, predicate-triggered profiles.* Profiles
shall be composed on three axes — jurisdiction, sector and
characteristic — and shall attach by trigger predicate evaluated
against twin facts (6.4). Each profile shall bind data points,
transforms, cryptographic suites, trust requirements, access policies,
presentation requirements and carrier requirements (6.3).

[[inv-I11]]
*Invariant I11 — no load-bearing proprietary layer.* The mount (the
profile interface), identity, semantics and trust shall be open ISO,
IEC, ITU or UN mechanisms. Any mapping to a proprietary twin
representation shall be informative at most and shall never be
load-bearing.

[[inv-I12]]
*Invariant I12 — enumeration resistance.* The system shall be
verifiable without being browsable. Resolution shall be by identity
only; traversal shall require per-edge rights; logs shall anchor
commitments (hashes), never facts; blind edges shall be the default
where households or sites would otherwise be revealed; proof of binding
shall be distinct from knowledge of parent; and recalls shall be
predicate-based (7.7).

[[inv-operation]]
=== Operation and resilience invariants (I13 to I14)

[[inv-I13]]
*Invariant I13 — degradation ladder.* Payloads shall be served in three
tiers — Tier A (carrier-embedded minimum), Tier B (served) and Tier C
(archival) — and degradation between tiers shall be explicit through
freshness verdicts (10.4), never a silent pass. Availability shall be
decoupled from origin servers through transparency-log inclusion
proofs, mirrors and archives (10.7).

[[inv-I14]]
*Invariant I14 — continuity.* The framework shall provide succession
mechanisms for hosts, trust authorities, schemes, algorithms, profiles
and regimes, such that identities outlive their issuers and hosts
(11.7).

[[cl-framework-layers]]
=== Layer architecture

The framework is organized in the following layers. Each layer is
neutral with respect to the layers above it; no region is the universal
envelope.

.Layer architecture of the framework
[cols="10,22,38,30",options="header"]
|===
| Layer | Name | Content | Mechanism (stewardship)

| L0 | Identity (global) | one unique product identifier per subject, granularity model/batch/item, one physical carrier | ISO/IEC 15459 issuance; bridges to GS1, Handle, Ecode, MA and further registered schemes
| L1 | Neutral core passport | minimal skeleton: product ID, passport ID, status, timestamps, profile manifest, event log, signatures; deliberately free of any jurisdiction's fields | this document
| L2 | Profiles | registered, versioned objects binding data points, transforms, suites, trust, access, presentation, carriers and trigger predicates | this document; registration per ISO 19135
| L3 | Semantic registry | every data point defined once, in federated registers with governed item lifecycle | ISO 19135 federated registers (FERIN), seeded by trade-data element dictionaries; CDDAL content technology
| L4 | Trust | per-profile attestations chaining to the jurisdiction's own trust framework; global cross-recognition via threshold master lists | SIGNATIF (Clause 11)
| L5 | Resolution | resolver linksets keyed by profile context, role, language and request region; one carrier, many destinations | GS1 Digital Link and IETF RFC 9264 linksets; federated resolvers
| L6 | Regional serving | in-country DPP service provision where data-residency law requires it | national hosts, regional service providers
|===

The resolution chain shall proceed: carrier URI → federated resolver →
linkset (profile context × role × language) → hosting endpoint(s) →
render plus manifest, with registry and dictionary lookups performed in
parallel. Mirrors and caches shall carry as-of stamps.

[[cl-framework-roles]]
=== Role separation and authority model

Issuance, hosting, identity, anchoring, archiving and trust are
separable duties (I7). The framework shall recognize the following
roles; one party may hold several, and none is implied by another.

.Role separation
[cols="25,45,30",options="header"]
|===
| Role | Responsibility | Notes

| Issuing authority | signs passport issuance and derivation events: manufacturer (primary), transformer (splits, combines), remanufacturer (new types), registrar (type passports), qualified attestor (commissioning of orphan objects), regulator-direct (exceptional) | issuance is open; recognition is graded by each profile's trust binding — unsigned self-issued passports remain valid for voluntary provenance and insufficient for regulated claims
| Hosting authority | serves renders: DPP service providers (main and back-up), manufacturer source systems, custodian-held state, device edge, national in-country hosts | republication never transfers authority
| Registrar | identity, type registrations, profile applicability bindings, carrier replacement events | registry holds identity and resolution, not state
| Anchor | transparency logs: the tamper-evident spine | M-of-K quorum, no single log
| Archivist | Tier C persistence | OAIS-style archival, media refresh
| Trust authority | roots, trust lists, accreditation, revocation | threshold groups; Clause 11
|===

Verification classes served by the framework include border and market
surveillance authorities (Tier A offline, acceptance policies),
commercial verifiers, recyclers and end-of-life operators, consumers,
machines, auditors and certifiers, insurers and financiers.

[[cl-framework-seams]]
=== Seam register

Layers join at seams; failures localize at seams. The framework shall
treat the following seam register as the contract inventory of the
architecture. Conformance claims shall identify the seams covered.

.Seam register
[cols="8,25,67",options="header"]
|===
| Seam | Join | Contract

| S1 | carrier ↔ resolver | code-format translation; 1D-to-2D migration coexistence; carrier URI integrity
| S2 | resolver ↔ hosts | mirrors, national intermediary layers, dark identifiers; linkset routing
| S3 | core ↔ profiles | the mount — where neutrality is enforced (6.1)
| S4 | profile ↔ registry | data-point reference resolution, version pinning at verification time
| S5 | registry ↔ registry | register federation, canonicalization of definitions across hosts, cross-register mappings as registered items
| S6 | event log ↔ transparency logs | commitment anchoring, salt discipline (8.3)
| S7 | issuer credentials ↔ profile trust lists | delegation DAG, roots, master list (11.6)
| S8 | transforms ↔ engines | executable-rule packages; determinism; version pinned at verification time (9.6)
| S9 | parent ↔ child passports | installation edges, blind modes, mixed-profile children (7.6, 7.7)
| S10 | device ↔ system | edge commit/reveal, contact dumps (10.6)
| S11 | Tier A ↔ Tier B | embedded minimum versus served; staleness marking (10.1 to 10.4)
| S12 | passport ↔ adjacent regimes | customs, e-invoicing, supply-chain event and traceability vocabularies, museum provenance ontologies, financial messaging
|===

[NOTE]
====
The defect register of the European DPP corpus (Annex A, <<unidpp-audit>>)
classifies published defects as, without exception, failures at one or
more of these seams.
====

[[cl-framework-source-invariants]]
=== Source invariants

Invariants I1 to I14 derive from the following source invariants, which
state the requirement-derivation form of the framework. Where a source
invariant is elaborated by a later clause, the clause reference is
given.

.Source invariants
[cols="8,52,20",options="header"]
|===
| # | Source invariant | Elaborated in

| 1 | International interoperability — cross-verification without adopting foreign rules (verify any jurisdiction's view under your own anchors) | 11.4, 11.6
| 2 | National data sovereignty — each jurisdiction governs its profiles, trust, crypto and, where law requires, in-country serving | 6.8, 11.2
| 3 | Digital twin as the interactive layer; passport as its legal projection | 6.1
| 4 | State-topology freedom — edge segments, centralized providers, manufacturer source systems or any combination; the twin is the union of custodian-held event segments joined by identity and commitments | 10.6
| 5 | Offline-online combination — Tier A carrier minimum, Tier B served, Tier C archival; explicit freshness verdicts | Clause 10
| 6 | Static and dynamic projections — profiles declare per-element provenance and freshness requirements | 6.6
| 7 | Point-in-time versus dynamic state — two authoritative query modes: live view (freshness-labelled) and notarized as-of snapshot (log-anchored); legal purpose selects the mode | 8.6
| 8 | Signed and unsigned — trust is graded: every element/event carries a trust marker; profiles set minimum signing per data class | Clause 11
| 9 | Profile-set growth is dated binding, not new identity — adding a profile to an existing model is a registry applicability event with an effective window (including retroactive flags); new model identity only on technical type change | 6.7, 7.4
| 10 | Issuance-granularity rule — manufacturer is an actor, not a level; model is a registered type definition that defines semantics for instance elements; passports are issued wherever a legal duty attaches; instance passports reference the exact type version and configuration vector; model-contains-model defines potential composition, instance references actual child instances | 7.4
| 11 | Executable-rule placement — a profile may bind an executable regulation package as its transform, decision and conformity rule set, carrying clause-level provenance identifiers; conformance suites run the same packages | 9.6
|===

[NOTE]
====
The travel-document analogy holds for trust, issuance and the offline
tier: the electronic passport for trust and issuance, the machine-
readable zone for the offline minimum, and the international driving
permit for profiles — a national document with convention renditions.
Unlike travel documents, the scene is a live twin and the profile set
grows.
====
