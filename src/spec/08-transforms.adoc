[[cl-transforms]]
== Transformation algebra

[[cl-transform-dag]]
=== The provenance DAG

Passports are nodes in a provenance directed acyclic graph;
transformations are typed events on its edges, pointing from inputs to
outputs (I4, I5). The framework shall provide navigation in both
directions:

a) *trace-up* — the ancestors of a passport: everything it derives
   from (composition of chains);

b) *trace-down* — the descendants: where a passport's remainder or
   outputs went;

c) *recall set* — a root plus all its descendants, as the routing set
   applied after a local predicate match (7.7 e);

d) *taints-of* — the union of taints of a passport and all its
   ancestors (9.7).

Roll-up views over deep graphs (aggregate carbon, recycled content)
shall be computed from references, and may be served as signed
roll-up attestations over a committed traversal set (a commitment to
the child-passport version set), verifiable without fetching every
passport and spot-checkable; caches are optional and always as-of
stamped. Availability shall be decoupled from origin servers through
transparency-log inclusion proofs.

[[cl-transform-split]]
=== Split (1 → N)

A split records the division of one passport's quantity among children,
recorded in the parent's log by its custodian. Children shall be new
passports carrying a derived-from reference and quantity carve-outs.

a) *Remainder semantics.* The sum of child carve-outs shall not exceed
   the parent's available quantity: `Σ children ≤ parent`. An over-carve
   shall be rejected.

b) *Dimension consistency.* Every carve-out shall be of the same
   dimension as the parent quantity (9.5); negative carve-outs shall be
   rejected.

c) *Remainder.* The remainder — the parent's quantity minus the sum of
   carve-outs, expressed exactly in the parent's own unit — stays with
   the parent.

d) *Consumption.* When the remainder is exactly zero, the parent
   transitions to `consumed` or `transformed` (8.4): historical, still
   verifiable, still needed for as-of queries.

[[cl-transform-combine]]
=== Combine and transformation (N → 1)

A combine or transformation records the production of one output
passport from many inputs. The output passport's issuance event shall
carry *input references*, each being (passport, quantity taken, as-of
state hash of that passport at reference time), so that the new
passport hash-links its sources (a merge topology over the DAG).

a) Output quantities shall be *new measured facts* with provenance
   "computed by transformation event E" — never copies of input
   quantities.

b) *Mass balance.* `in − out = loss`, where loss shall be
   non-negative and auditable as process loss; a negative balance
   (output exceeding input) shall be rejected.

c) *Availability checks.* Each input reference shall be checked against
   the as-of available quantity of that input; over-consumption of any
   input shall be rejected, as shall dimension mismatch between output
   and inputs.

d) Inputs transition to `consumed` or `transformed` (8.4 b).

e) *Stamp-context inheritance.* Stamp quantity contexts ("verified
   500 g of A at T", 11.3) attached to the inputs shall be inherited by
   the output.

f) *Taint inheritance.* The output's taint set shall be the union of
   the inputs' taint sets (9.7); mass-balance accounting shall carry
   taint explicitly.

g) *Accreditation.* Only actors accredited for the regulated claims
   involved may issue derived passports for regulated claims (the
   notified-actor pattern of <<en18239>> as an informative EU-profile
   reference); unaccredited issuance of regulated claims shall be a
   conformance failure.

[[cl-transform-eow]]
=== End-of-waste and decompose

a) *End-of-waste* is the legal state transition where a material
   re-qualifies for a passport: the event shall carry an evidence
   reference (the applicable end-of-waste criteria and decision) and
   the output carve-outs, and shall transition the subject's status to
   `end-of-waste` or re-enter it as `issued` (secondary raw material)
   per the applicable regime.

b) *Decompose* (E13) is the inverse transformation at end-of-life,
   1 → N, producing material passports; it shall be appended by a
   recycler accredited for the claims, and records whether the actor is
   accredited.

c) The material state machine closes the loop: natural resource →
   extracted lot (batch passport plus extraction profile) →
   transformation chain → product → waste status → *end-of-waste event
   = new passport issuance* → secondary raw material → re-entry. Mass
   balance and taint propagate through the whole loop (cradle-to-
   cradle, not cradle-to-grave).

[[cl-transform-units]]
=== Units and quantities

Every measured quantity shall carry a registered unit; values without
registered units shall not participate in transformations (the
quantity chain: ISO 80000 → units registry).

a) *Unit registry.* Units shall be defined in a registry seeded from
   ISO 80000 and bound to a units registry namespace (UnitsML /
   UnitsDB), each definition carrying the dimension and an exact
   rational factor to the dimension's canonical unit. The framework's
   seed registry shall provide at least: mass (kg, g, mg, t, lb),
   energy (J, kJ, MJ, Wh, kWh), volume (m3, l, ml), count (count), and
   dimensionless (ratio, percent), with their exact factors.

b) *No conflicting redefinition.* A second registration of an existing
   unit token with different dimension or factors shall be rejected;
   identical re-registration shall be idempotent.

c) *Canonical amounts.* Quantity arithmetic (sum, difference,
   comparison, conversion) shall be performed in exact arithmetic on
   canonical amounts (amount × factor), and remainders and losses
   shall be expressed exactly in the requested unit.

d) *Dimension discipline.* Additions, subtractions and comparisons
   shall be dimension-checked; dimension mismatch shall be rejected
   (9.2 b, 9.3 c).

e) *Deterministic conversion.* Conversions shall be deterministic and
   exact (for example kWh → MJ); profiles shall bind conversion factors
   from the registry, never invent them.

[[cl-transform-registered]]
=== Registered transforms

Transforms are first-class, versioned, registered objects with
effective dates and legal citation (5.9). The transform taxonomy shall
comprise at least:

a) *unit conversion* — deterministic, factors per ISO 80000 and the
   units registry;

b) *derivation* — deterministic arithmetic over same-twin elements;

c) *aggregation* — cross-child roll-ups, methodology-bound: the
   transform cites the method standard (for example a carbon-footprint
   method) and never invents one;

d) *classification / binning* — threshold tables and code-list
   mappings (for example energy-class scales versus star ratings versus
   national labels);

e) *localization mapping* — registered code-list correspondences (waste
   codes, tariff nomenclatures).

The following design rules shall hold.

f) *Determinism* — an inspector shall be able to recompute the
   classification from the canonical facts and the cited rules.

g) *Explicit rounding* — rounding and significant-digit rules shall be
   published by the transform.

h) *Measurement-uncertainty propagation* per the GUM, and *conformity
   decision rules* (guard-banding per the shared-risk versus guarded
   rules of the applicable regime) for boundary measurements, shall be
   published by the transform: a measurement near a class boundary may
   be classified differently under different decision rules, and the
   rule is law.

i) *Canonical storage* — the twin stores canonical measured values in
   canonical units only, never pre-transformed duplicates, so rule
   changes re-project rather than re-enter data.

j) *Executable form* — the normative transform-expression language for
   regulations-as-executable-models shall carry clause-level
   provenance identifiers (URNs), so that every derived value traces to
   the exact legal paragraph it implements. Embedded ad-hoc scripts in
   specifications are not conforming transform objects; transforms are
   governed registry objects with a conformance suite (Clause 12).

k) *Trust split* — attest inputs (measurement events); transforms
   verify deterministically for free; attest outputs only where the
   law certifies the classification itself.

[[cl-transform-taint]]
=== Taint propagation

Marking a passport fraudulent or otherwise tainted is a *graph event*,
not only a trust-list event (I9).

a) *Taint kinds.* The framework shall provide at least: `revoked`,
   `compromised`, `misissued`, `fraud`, `non-conformant`,
   `contaminated`, `legal-hold`, plus registered extensions
   (`other:<token>`).

b) *Retroactivity.* The revocation reason determines retroactivity:
   prospective reasons (key compromise after a time, cessation,
   supersession) leave prior as-of verifications valid; retroactive
   reasons (`misissued`, `fraudulent issuance`, authority compromised
   during a window) void validity ab initio — time-stamping does not
   protect them. Distrust declarations shall carry an explicit window
   [start, end]; passports inside the window are voided, outside it
   re-validated.

c) *Propagation.* Taint shall propagate downstream through the
   provenance DAG (the same machinery as recalls, different cause):
   tainting derived passports, recomputing affected claims (a
   recycled-content claim built on stolen material collapses),
   notifying custodians, possibly suspending products. The taint set
   effective on a passport is the union of its own taints and its
   ancestors' taints.

d) *Adjudication.* Issuer misconduct leads to a status-invalidation
   cascade with adjudication; adjudicated outcomes are themselves
   logged events.
