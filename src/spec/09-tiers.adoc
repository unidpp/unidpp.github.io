[[cl-tiers]]
== Payload tiers and offline operation

[[cl-tiers-ladder]]
=== The degradation ladder

The framework shall serve payloads in three tiers (I13):

* *Tier A* — the carrier-embedded minimum viable passport (10.2),
 sized to the data carrier and verifiable offline against pre-cached
 trust anchors;

* *Tier B* — the served full passport, resolvable per profile,
 including device-served endpoints (10.6);

* *Tier C* — the archival record (persistence beyond operator
 lifetime, archival refresh).

The framework shall fix exactly which subset of fields is Tier A
(10.2); every field of the Tier-A subset shall be mandatory — there
shall be no optional-field shrinking, because shrinking would silently
degrade the offline minimum. Stale or offline data shall degrade
explicitly through freshness verdicts (10.4) and shall never silently
pass. The degradation ladder is the framework's interface to deployed
reality: a silent S0 product shall be fully servable on the ladder with
zero device participation (6.5 b).

[[cl-tiers-tiera]]
=== Tier-A payload

The Tier-A payload shall comprise exactly the following fields, all
mandatory:

.Tier-A payload fields
[cols="28,50,22",options="header"]
|===
| Field | Content | Source rule

| product identifier | scheme-agnostic product identifier in canonical display form (7.1) | identity
| resolver URI | the URI for Tier-B resolution | resolution
| passport identifier | the passport URN (7.2) | identity
| economic-operator identifier | the EO identifier | issuer
| status | the current status (8.4) | replayed from the log
| safety | the critical safety / recall flag: none, recall-active, security-flagged | replayed from the log (recall precedence)
| validity | the validity interval of the passport | issuance
| as-of stamp | when this projection was computed | the last event time of the log
| log head | the log head commitment (offline chain anchoring) | the log head
| signatures | multi-suite compressed signature framing (11.2) | signing layer
|===

The Tier-A view shall be constructible from the event log by replay:
status and safety flag replayed from the log, as-of stamp set to the
last event time, log head set to the log's head commitment.

[[cl-tiers-budget]]
=== Carrier budgets

Tier-A payloads shall be budgeted against the capacity of the physical
carrier.

a) *QR capacity model.* For QR carriers, the byte-mode data capacity
 per symbol version 1 to 40 and error-correction level L, M, Q or H
 per ISO/IEC 18004 shall be used as the budget table (for example
 version 40 holds 2 953 bytes at L and 1 273 bytes at H; version 1
 holds 17 bytes at L).

b) *Deterministic encoding.* The Tier-A payload shall be encoded as a
 deterministic tagged byte sequence (fixed field tags for the fields
 of 10.2), with signature slots framed by suite code and key
 identifier.

c) *Conservative projection.* Budgeting shall use the *projected
 length*: signature placeholders counted at each suite's canonical
 signature length — 64 bytes for ECDSA P-256 and SM2; 2 420 bytes for
 ML-DSA-44; 3 309 bytes for ML-DSA-65; 4 627 bytes for ML-DSA-87 — so
 that a frame-only payload is budgeted as if fully signed.

d) *Version selection.* The smallest carrier version that holds the
 projected payload at the configured error-correction level shall be
 selected; if none within the configured maximum version holds it,
 the packing shall fail as over-budget.

e) *Over-budget handling.* An over-budget payload shall be rejected
 with an explicit error; the remedies are to shrink the resolver URI
 or to drop signature suites. Silent truncation shall not occur.

f) *Default configuration.* The default error-correction level is M
 with maximum version 40; configurations may be stricter.

g) *Other carriers.* Data Matrix carriers (ISO/IEC 16022), RFID air
 interfaces (ISO/IEC 18000-3 and ISO/IEC 18000-63) and near-field
 communication (ISO/IEC 18092) shall be budgeted against their
 respective capacity models under the same projection and rejection
 rules.

[[cl-tiers-freshness]]
=== Freshness verdicts

Freshness shall be evaluated relative to the projection's as-of stamp
and the profile's freshness requirement (6.6). The freshness verdict
shall take exactly one of the following values:

* `fresh` — an as-of stamp is present and, for fresh-within
 requirements, its age at verification time is within the window;

* `stale` — an as-of stamp is present but older than the window
 (degrade explicitly);

* `indeterminate` — no as-of information at all (offline with no
 anchor).

Static and none requirements shall never evaluate stale: an as-of
stamp, however old, evaluates fresh; absence evaluates indeterminate.
Freshness verdicts shall be displayed as verdicts (staleness is a
meaningful, displayable property), never silently dropped.

[[cl-tiers-verdicts]]
=== Verdict outcomes on the ladder

Tier-A verification shall conclude in exactly one of three outcomes:

* *pass*;

* *degraded*, with an explicit cause: stale data (with the as-of
 stamp), no freshness evidence, offline with no anchor, signatures
 framed only, or coverage incomplete (with the missing data points);

* *fail*, with an explicit cause: broken chain or anchor mismatch.

Integrity failures shall outrank degradations: a broken chain or an
anchor mismatch fails the verdict regardless of freshness or coverage
state. Framed-only signature slots (signatures present as placeholders)
shall degrade the verdict explicitly rather than pass it.

[[cl-tiers-edge]]
=== Edge segments

The device is a tier of the passport system, not a client (source
invariant 4 of 5.11). Some lifecycle events originate on-device
(battery cycles, usage, firmware) and the device may be offline by
circumstance or unwilling by policy (privacy, trade secrets). The
*commit-now, reveal-later, never-contradict* rules shall hold:

a) the device shall publish commitments — a hash of its local
 append-only event log prefix, signed by a device-bound key holding
 device credentials — plus whatever events it chooses to reveal;

b) future reveals shall be inclusion-consistent with past commitments
 (the transparency-log pattern applied to device logs: the device can
 withhold but cannot retroactively lie);

c) the server-side passport is the set of operator-attested events; the
 device segment is the set of device-measured events; the union is
 joined by identity and commitment hashes;

d) freshness verdicts shall degrade when commitments go stale relative
 to the profile's fresh-within window;

e) the device may serve its own segment (near-field, Bluetooth, local
 resolver): a device-served endpoint is a legitimate Tier-B endpoint;

f) contact dumps (capability class S2) are first-class reveals: the
 logged-contact device dumps on physical read.

The twin is the union of custodian-held event segments joined by
identity and commitments; the framework fixes segment ownership and
consistency rules, not topology (state-topology freedom).

[[cl-tiers-outage]]
=== Outage doctrine

Resilience is layered; for each failure mode the framework shall
provide the following minimum behaviours.

.Outage doctrine
[cols="30,70",options="header"]
|===
| Failure mode | Behaviour

| client offline | Tier A with cached anchors, as-of marked
| origin server down | mirrors and caches; log inclusion proofs; Tier C archive serving
| resolver outage | federated alternates, national mirrors, cached linksets; the carrier retains identity and resolution is reproducible
| host defunct | registry marking, resolver redirection, hosting-handover ceremony, escrow (11.7)
| registry outage | per-version items immutable and mirrorable; entries log-anchored
| log outage | M-of-K quorum across independent logs (11.6)
| trust authority outage or compromise | threshold roots, rotation with continuity, revocation-reason taxonomy with windows, emergency threshold votes (11.5, 11.6)
| authority removal | affected profiles quarantined; verification under other anchors and in evidentiary mode
| scheme-operator failure | IANA-style succession obligations recorded at registration; dormant-identifier continuity (11.7)
| jurisdictional disconnection | cached anchors and logs sustain verification; evidentiary mode; reconnection ceremonies
| long-term migration | post-quantum schedules via the agility registry; format migration because serializations are renders of the core; media migration via Tier C archival refresh
|===

Availability shall be decoupled from origin servers: transparency-log
inclusion proofs allow a verifier to conclude without the issuing
service being reachable (I13).
